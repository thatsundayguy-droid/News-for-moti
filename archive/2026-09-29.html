Global consumer: Frugality is the new normal

The global consumer picture in late 2026 is defined by caution -- this isn't a cyclical pullback, it's a structural reset:

  • Non-food retail faces projected declines of 24 percentage points globally; only groceries show expected volume growth.
  • Private label adoption has gone mainstream -- consumers across income brackets are switching from national brands to own-brand alternatives.
  • Creator trust is now primary: authenticity and genuine community connections beat traditional advertising with younger cohorts.

The strategic implication: brands must deliver clear value propositions and build authentic trust, not rely on promotional cycles.

CFA Corner
Concept of the Day — EV/EBITDA Multiple

The EV/EBITDA (Enterprise Value to Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation) multiple is one of the most widely used valuation tools in equity analysis -- particularly relevant for capital-intensive industries and cross-border comparisons where capital structure and tax regimes differ.

EV / EBITDA  =  (Market Cap + Debt − Cash) ÷ EBITDA

Why use it over P/E? Because EV/EBITDA is capital-structure neutral -- it looks at the whole enterprise (equity + debt), not just equity holders. This makes it far more useful when comparing companies with different debt levels, or when a company's net income is distorted by interest charges or tax loss carry-forwards.

Key nuance: EBITDA excludes capital expenditure -- so for asset-heavy businesses (telecoms, utilities, infrastructure), analysts often prefer EV/EBIT or EV/EBITDA minus capex (i.e., EV/EBITDA-Capex) to avoid overstating cash generation. A low multiple relative to sector peers may signal undervaluation, but always check whether EBITDA margins are sustainable.
